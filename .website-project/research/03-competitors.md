## What buyers already expect

A trial or a low-friction start, a Xero and QuickBooks connection, multi-entity reporting, some form of elimination, and a claim about time saved. AI is now on the homepage of Fathom, Spotlight, Joiin, Mayday, and Lucanet.

## What to adapt, without copying

- Say who the page is for in the first screen. Fathom and Joiin split “businesses” and “advisers”. GATHER already has those two audiences and should make them this obvious.
- Put the proof next to the claim: a named review, a real number you can stand behind, or a product picture. Do not invent the number.
- Show the month-end job, not only the report. Mayday and Translucent sell the close. GATHER’s intercompany workflow is the answer, and it should be visible before the fold on that product page.

## The gap GATHER can own

Joiin, Fathom, and Spotlight lead with reporting and packs. Mayday and Translucent lead with close tasks. Lucanet leads with the enterprise suite. GATHER already sits between them: intercompany control, group reporting, and group planning on the ledgers scaling groups actually use, including the harder consolidation cases. That combination is the position to keep. It is credible because the product pages and knowledge articles already describe it.

## Three moves

1. Make the first screen name the reader (finance team or fractional CFO) and the job (close the group without a spreadsheet). Effort: low. Benefit: clearer than a feature list.
2. Keep a single comparison page per rival, on the URL that is already indexed, and answer “which job is ours?” in the first paragraph. Effort: medium. Benefit: captures people already comparing tools.
3. Add a plain “how the close works” path from intercompany to reporting to planning. Effort: medium. Benefit: matches the way Mayday and Translucent explain the month, without becoming a close-only tool.

Guide

Financial consolidation for Xero and QuickBooks Online groups

Learn how to consolidate Xero and QuickBooks group accounts, from account mapping and currency translation to eliminations and working paper review.

On this page

Financial consolidation brings a parent company and its subsidiaries together as one economic entity. It goes beyond adding up company reports: the finance team must also address intercompany activity, ownership, accounting policies and currencies.

This guide explains a practical workflow for CFOs and controllers managing groups whose entities use Xero or QuickBooks Online.

What financial consolidation involves

Each company maintains its own accounting records. Group reporting then answers a different question: what does the combined business earn, own and owe when transactions within the group are removed?

A reliable process connects the entity accounts to the reported group result through identifiable adjustments. Readers should be able to distinguish the underlying balances, the consolidation entries and the final figures.

This guide focuses on the monthly reporting process. Statutory financial statements also require the applicable presentation, disclosure and other reporting requirements to be met. The detailed accounting depends on the framework used, including IFRS or the relevant local GAAP.

Define the group and reporting period

Start with the consolidation perimeter. Identify the parent, the entities it controls, and the dates on which control began or ended. Under IFRS 10, control is the basis for consolidation; ownership percentage alone is not a complete test.

Keep an ownership schedule recording direct holdings, intermediate parents and changes during the year. An acquisition partway through the period creates different requirements from a subsidiary that has been owned throughout. Associates and joint arrangements require a separate assessment.

Agree the reporting date, timetable and responsibilities. A technically correct consolidation can still mislead if one entity has not completed its accruals or another has supplied the wrong period.

Collect complete and consistent entity data

Connect or collect the accounting information for each entity and confirm the reporting cut-off. Check that the source data reconciles to the entity trial balance and that opening balances and comparatives are available where required.

Check which changes have been made since the previous reporting run. A late entity journal can affect consolidated profit, intercompany matching and a previously reviewed working paper.

Map accounts to a common reporting structure

Groups do not need identical entity charts of accounts to report consistently. They do need a controlled mapping from each local account to the appropriate group reporting line.

Example

One entity may use separate software subscription and hosting accounts while another uses a single IT expense account. Map these deliberately into the group structure, retaining the detail needed for analysis.

Review new and unmapped accounts every period. A report that balances can still be incomplete if a source account has been excluded or mapped to the wrong line. Accounting policy differences may also require adjustments; mapping alone does not resolve them.

Translate foreign operations into the reporting currency

Where functional currencies differ from the group presentation currency, apply the translation rules required by the reporting framework. Under IAS 21, for foreign operations outside hyperinflationary circumstances, assets and liabilities generally use the closing rate, while income and expenses use transaction-date rates. An average can be used when it reasonably approximates those rates.

Translation differences require their own explanation. They should not become an unexplained balancing figure. Distinguish translation of a foreign operation from exchange gains and losses already recorded on transactions in an entity ledger.

Retain the rates, their direction and the source used. Review historical equity and acquisition-related balances separately where relevant.

Reconcile intercompany positions before eliminating them

Compare reciprocal receivables and payables and investigate differences before applying elimination entries. Common causes include missing postings, different cut-offs, account coding and exchange-rate effects.

Reconciliation checks whether the entities agree. Elimination removes the internal activity from the group result. The two tasks are connected, but an elimination does not explain or correct a missing entry in the underlying books.

Also consider internal income and expenses, loans and interest, dividends and unrealised profits included in assets. Avoid assuming that eliminating intercompany balance sheet accounts completes every adjustment.

Record ownership and other consolidation adjustments

Eliminate the parent’s investment against the relevant subsidiary equity and account for acquisition-related items under the applicable framework. Depending on the facts, these can include goodwill, fair-value adjustments and non-controlling interests.

For a controlled subsidiary that is not wholly owned, full consolidation generally includes all its assets, liabilities, income and expenses, with non-controlling interests presented separately. It is not simply a percentage roll-up.

Keep group-only adjustments distinct from source-ledger corrections. Record the reason, effective period, calculation and whether the entry recurs or reverses.

A simple example of the bridge to group revenue

Worked example

Assume a wholly owned subsidiary has been controlled for the full period. Both companies report in sterling. The parent records £1 million of revenue, including a £100,000 management charge to the subsidiary. The subsidiary records £600,000 of external revenue and the matching expense.

Revenue bridge
Revenue bridgeAmount
Parent revenue£1,000,000
Subsidiary revenue£600,000
Aggregated revenue£1,600,000
Eliminate internal management charge(£100,000)
Consolidated revenue£1,500,000

The matching £100,000 expense is also eliminated. This paired elimination reduces group revenue and expenses by the same amount, leaving group profit unchanged. Other adjustments could affect profit; this example isolates one internal service charge.

Review the working papers and explain the result

Review the bridge from mapped entity figures to consolidated balances. Check significant movements, journal completeness, intercompany residuals and the relationship between opening and closing equity.

The reviewer should be able to open a reported amount, identify the contributing companies and accounts, inspect the adjustments and understand the backing calculations. Preserve the reviewed version and document any subsequent changes.

The objective is a repeatable close in which the finance team can explain the group result and reproduce the supporting work.

Frequently asked questions

Is combining trial balances the same as consolidation?

No. Combining trial balances is a starting point. Consolidation also addresses internal activity, investment and equity eliminations, ownership and other required adjustments.

Can a group keep different charts of accounts?

Yes. A common group reporting structure can be supported by mappings from different local charts, provided completeness and classification are controlled.

Does consolidation replace the entity accounts?

No. Entity accounts remain the underlying records. Group adjustments should be clearly distinguished from entries that need to be corrected in an entity ledger.

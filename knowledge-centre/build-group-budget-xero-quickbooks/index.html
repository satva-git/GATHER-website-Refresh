How to build a group budget for Xero and QuickBooks Online businesses

A group budget brings the plans of different parts of a business into a shared financial view. For groups using Xero or QuickBooks Online, the practical challenge is to align the reporting structure, periods and assumptions so that planned performance can be compared with actual results. Start with a common format and a clear definition of what each contribution includes.

Decide what the group budget needs to answer

Agree the decisions the budget will support. Leadership may need to assess profitability, set spending limits, allocate resources or understand the expected year-end financial position.

These needs determine the level of detail to collect.

Identify the legal entities within scope and the management views needed, such as business unit, region or function. These views can overlap, so they should not automatically become separate amounts to add together.

Use a common reporting structure

Build the budget around reporting lines that can also be used for actuals. Agree definitions for revenue, direct costs, overheads and the relevant balance sheet accounts.

Local companies can retain different charts of accounts. What matters is that their actual balances map consistently into the group structure and that the budget uses the same definitions.

For example, if hosting costs are budgeted as a direct cost but actuals appear in administrative expenses, the reported gross margin variance will include a classification difference.

Resolve that before asking managers to explain trading performance.

Give each contribution a clear owner and scope

Ask contributors to specify what their submission covers and which costs are excluded.

State who owns shared services, central overheads and assumptions that affect several parts of the group.

An operating unit may budget external revenue and direct delivery costs while the finance team budgets central costs. This can work well if the boundaries are explicit.

 It fails when both parties assume the other has included an expense.

Use a simple register listing each budget contribution, its owner, reporting lines and due date.

Build monthly figures from understandable assumptions

Use realistic timing rather than dividing every annual figure by twelve. Revenue may be seasonal; recruitment, insurance renewals and capital purchases may occur in specific months.

Retain the assumptions behind material amounts. These might include customer volumes, staffing dates, planned pay changes or contract renewals.

 Detailed modelling can be prepared separately, with reviewed figures then entered into the budgeting system.

Agree the currency and rate basis for each contribution. If reporting actuals at different rates, explain how FX affects the comparison.

An illustrative group budget roll up

Assume two operating units budget only external revenue and their own operating costs.

Central costs are held separately. All figures below are annual sterling amounts and exclude interest and tax.

ContributionRevenueOperating costsOperating profit
Unit A£1,000,000£700,000£300,000
Unit B£600,000£450,000£150,000
Central costs£0£120,000(£120,000)
Group budget£1,600,000£1,270,000£330,000

The group operating profit is £330,000. Before accepting it, check that central costs have not also been included in the unit submissions.

If the plan contains internal charges or trading, agree how these are reflected in the group view.

Adding contributions is not by itself evidence that all required budget adjustments have been made.

Include the planned financial position

A profit budget does not explain the expected balance sheet. Consider the effects of customer payment timing, supplier balances, inventory, capital expenditure and financing.

Check opening balances and reconcile planned movements to the closing position.

A business can budget a profit while also needing more cash to fund receivables or equipment.

Where supporting schedules are prepared outside the application, keep their assumptions aligned with the figures entered.

A balance sheet budget should not become a disconnected second exercise.

Review the plan and establish the comparison basis

  • Confirm that every required contribution is present.
  • Review missing costs, overlapping submissions and internal activity.
  • Test monthly timing and material assumptions.
  • Reconcile the group totals to the underlying contributions.
  • Identify the agreed budget version and retain its supporting schedules.

Once actuals are available, use the same reporting structure and periods to compare performance.

Explain changes in scope separately from ordinary operating variances.

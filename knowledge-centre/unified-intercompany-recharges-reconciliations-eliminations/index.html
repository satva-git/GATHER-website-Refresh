Unifying intercompany recharges, reconciliations and eliminations

Connecting centralised posting, intercompany reconciliation and consolidation eliminations helps finance teams reduce duplicated work, identify differences earlier and build more reliable group reporting.

Intercompany accounting works best when the transaction, the counterparty reconciliation and the consolidation treatment form one connected process.

A unified intercompany workflow connects three activities: posting corresponding entries across group companies, checking that their records agree, and eliminating internal balances and transactions from consolidated results.

Sharing the underlying configuration and supporting evidence across these activities can reduce repeated setup, improve visibility and make the month-end close easier to control.

For CFOs and financial controllers managing multi-entity groups, the benefit extends beyond faster recharges. It creates a clearer path from the original allocation decision to the final group accounts.

What are intercompany recharges, reconciliations and eliminations

These processes address different accounting needs. Connecting them strengthens control, but each retains a distinct purpose.

ProcessWhat it doesQuestion it answers
Intercompany recharge and centralised postingAllocates costs or records transactions between group companies, creating the relevant entries in their individual ledgers.Have we recorded the transaction correctly in each company?
Intercompany reconciliationCompares counterparty balances and transactions, identifying missing entries, timing differences, coding issues and currency differences.Do the companies’ records agree, and can we explain any differences?
Intercompany eliminationRemoves relevant internal balances, income, expenses and other internal effects when preparing consolidated accounts.Do the group accounts reflect the group as a single economic entity?

Posting does not replace reconciliation, and reconciliation does not replace elimination. A perfectly matched intercompany balance still needs the appropriate consolidation treatment.

Why separate intercompany workflows create extra work

Consider a parent company that pays for software used by several subsidiaries.

The finance team identifies the cost, calculates allocations, prepares recharge entries and posts them to the relevant companies.

It then reconciles the resulting balances and determines the eliminations needed for consolidation.

If each stage has its own spreadsheet or configuration, the team may repeatedly define the same relationships: which companies transact, which accounts they use, how allocations work and how the transaction should be eliminated.

Changes create further work. A new subsidiary, revised account code or updated recharge arrangement may need to be reflected in several places.

If one configuration is missed, the posting process can appear complete while reconciliation or consolidation remains incomplete.

The problem is often the handover between processes. A transaction is posted, but the finance team still has to establish whether both sides agree and whether the correct elimination is in place.

What does a unified intercompany workflow look like

A unified workflow connects the accounting relationship from setup through to group reporting.

Configure the recharge and its associated controls

Define the participating companies, allocation basis, account treatment, timing and any applicable mark-up.

At the same time, establish how the counterparty accounts will be reconciled and how the relevant balances and transactions will be eliminated.

Supporting documentation should explain the commercial basis for the recharge and how the allocation was calculated.

This makes the downstream controls part of setting up the relationship.

Post corresponding entries centrally

Centralised intercompany posting lets finance teams initiate the relevant entries across participating companies from one workflow.

For a straightforward recharge, each company records its own balanced entry. Coordinating both sides reduces the risk that one company records the transaction while the other misses it or uses a different amount.

Centralised posting can cover more than cost recharges. Depending on the system and configuration, it may also support other intercompany transactions and corrections.

Reconcile throughout the period

Reconciliation checks what has actually reached the ledgers, including transactions entered outside the central posting workflow.

Teams can investigate unmatched items and resolve differences before the close. This remains necessary even when postings are automated: later amendments, independently entered transactions and currency movements can still create discrepancies.

Validate the consolidation treatment

At consolidation, the team checks that relevant internal balances and transactions are eliminated and that the working papers agree with the reconciliation evidence.

The objective is a traceable connection between entity accounting, counterparty agreement and group reporting.

Worked example one recharge, three connected stages

Assume a parent company incurs £10,000 of external software costs and recharges £4,000 to a wholly owned subsidiary. Both companies use sterling. For simplicity, ignore tax, mark-ups and timing differences.

StageParent companySubsidiaryGroup effect
Original supplier costRecords £10,000 software expense.No entry.£10,000 external expense.
RechargeRecords £4,000 intercompany receivable and £4,000 recharge income.Records £4,000 software expense and £4,000 intercompany payable.Creates internal income, expense and balances.
ReconciliationConfirms its £4,000 receivable agrees with the subsidiary’s payable.Confirms its £4,000 payable agrees with the parent’s receivable.Establishes counterparty agreement.
EliminationConsolidation removes the internal receivable and recharge income.Consolidation removes the internal payable and recharged expense.Retains the original £10,000 external software cost.

The example assumes the parent presents the recharge as income. Some groups instead credit the original expense account, which changes the presentation and elimination mapping.

The elimination takes place at consolidation level. The receivable and payable remain in the individual companies’ books until settled or otherwise cleared.

What are the benefits of unifying intercompany accounting

Less duplicated configuration

A connected setup reduces the need to recreate company relationships and account mappings across recharge, reconciliation and consolidation processes.

This also makes changes easier to maintain as the group adds entities or revises its accounting structure.

Fewer avoidable posting differences

Coordinating both sides of a transaction reduces opportunities for inconsistent amounts, omitted counterpart entries and mismatched references.

The result is less corrective work for the reconciliation team.

Earlier identification of exceptions

Regular reconciliation brings missing postings and unexplained differences into view during the period.

Finance teams can spread investigation work across the month and arrive at close with fewer unresolved items.

More reliable consolidation inputs

Reconciled counterparty information provides a stronger basis for elimination review.

It helps teams distinguish a genuine posting error from a timing issue, currency difference or incorrect consolidation mapping, rather than using an unexplained adjustment to force balances to agree.

Clearer evidence and accountability

Keeping the allocation rationale, posting details, reconciliation explanations and consolidation treatment connected makes a transaction easier to review.

This can reduce the time spent reconstructing calculations and searching emails when a controller or auditor asks how a recharge was handled. Evidence should still be reviewed for completeness and accuracy.

Better visibility across the group

A relationship-level overview helps finance teams identify where controls have been configured, where differences remain and which intercompany relationships require attention.

That is especially useful when different people own entity accounting and group reporting, or when an outsourced finance team manages several groups.

A more repeatable close as the group grows

Additional entities introduce additional potential counterparty relationships. Currencies, shared services and acquisitions add further complexity.

A connected process helps the team accommodate that growth without recreating every handover manually. The scale of the time saving depends on transaction volumes, data quality and the existing process.

What should finance teams look for in intercompany software

Look beyond whether a platform offers three separate feature lists. Ask how the processes connect:

  • Can both sides of a transaction be created from one workflow?
  • Can recharge setup establish the associated reconciliation and elimination configuration?
  • Can the team identify missing controls across company relationships?
  • Can reconciliation capture transactions entered directly in the underlying ledgers?
  • Can reviewers trace allocations and exceptions to supporting evidence?
  • Can reconciliation results be validated against consolidation working papers?
  • Can the workflow accommodate the group’s currencies, account structures and reporting requirements?

A unified workflow still needs accounting judgement. Transfer-pricing policies, tax treatment, foreign-exchange differences and more complex eliminations require appropriate configuration and review.

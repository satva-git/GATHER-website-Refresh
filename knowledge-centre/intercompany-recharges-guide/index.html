Intercompany recharges explained

Journal entries cost allocations and worked examples. A practical guide for multi-entity groups using Xero and QuickBooks Online.

On this page

An intercompany recharge occurs when one company charges another company in the same group for costs or services. The finance team calculates the charge, records the corresponding entries in both companies and reconciles the resulting intercompany balances. When consolidated accounts are prepared, the appropriate internal balances and transactions are eliminated.

Common examples include shared software subscriptions, central payroll, management services, insurance and marketing costs.

For groups using separate Xero organisations or QuickBooks Online companies, the challenge is keeping the calculation, supporting evidence and accounting entries connected across every affected entity.

This guide explains the process through worked examples, from the original supplier bill to consolidation.

What is an intercompany recharge?

An intercompany recharge is a charge between related legal entities for an expense, service or other agreed cost. It places the appropriate cost in the company receiving the benefit and records the corresponding recovery or income in the company making the charge.

For example, a parent company might buy a software subscription used by three group companies. It retains its own share and recharges the remaining cost to the subsidiaries using an agreed allocation basis.

An intercompany recharge differs from a departmental allocation within one company: it affects separate sets of accounting records.

Common recharges and possible allocation bases
ExamplePossible allocation basis
Shared software subscriptionsActive users or licences assigned
Shared employeesRecorded time or an agreed measure of activity
Office costsFloor area or occupancy
Central finance and HR servicesRelevant service usage or another supportable driver
Group marketing costsCampaign beneficiaries or attributable activity

The allocation basis should reflect the arrangement and be supportable. A convenient percentage is not automatically an appropriate allocation method.

What is the difference between a recharge, an allocation and a settlement?

An allocation determines each company's share of a cost. A recharge records the charge between companies. Settlement clears the amount owed, usually through payment or an agreed offset.

From allocation to elimination
StageQuestion answeredExample
AllocationHow much should each company bear?Subsidiary B uses 30% of the licences.
RechargeWhat should be recorded between the companies?Parent A charges B £3,600.
SettlementHow is the amount owed cleared?B pays A £3,600.
ReconciliationDo the records agree, or are differences explained?A’s receivable agrees with B’s payable.
EliminationWhat internal activity must be removed from group accounts?Remove the reciprocal balance and internal income and expense.

Recording a recharge does not mean it has been paid. Eliminating it on consolidation does not settle the legal entities' balances.

How do you calculate an intercompany recharge?

Start with the eligible cost base, allocate it using the agreed driver and apply any mark-up required by the group's policy.

Recharge = allocated eligible cost × (1 + applicable mark-up percentage).

Without a mark-up, the recharge equals the allocated cost. Where a mark-up applies, the cost base and pricing method need to be established under the relevant policy; not every cost should automatically attract the same treatment.

Document the source costs, beneficiaries, allocation basis, period and pricing treatment so another person can reproduce the calculation.

For more on pricing policy, see Transfer pricing and intercompany accounting.

Worked example: allocating a shared software bill between three companies

Worked example

Parent A receives a £12,000 software bill covering one month. Licence usage supports the allocation below. For this illustration, all companies use sterling, the service is consumed in the month, no mark-up applies and VAT and other taxes are excluded. The example uses a separate recharge-income account in Parent A; account names and gross-versus-net presentation depend on the facts and applicable accounting policies.

Allocation of the £12,000 software bill
CompanyShareCost to bear
Parent A50%£6,000
Subsidiary B30%£3,600
Subsidiary C20%£2,400
Total100%£12,000

Step 1: record the external supplier bill

Parent A initially records the full bill:

Parent A: supplier bill
Parent A accountDebitCredit
Software expense£12,000—
External supplier payable—£12,000

If the supplier is subsequently paid, Parent A debits the supplier payable and credits bank. That payment is separate from the intercompany recharge.

Step 2: record the recharges in Parent A

Parent A charges £3,600 to B and £2,400 to C:

Parent A: recharges to B and C
Parent A accountDebitCredit
Intercompany receivable from B£3,600—
Intercompany receivable from C£2,400—
Intercompany recharge income—£6,000

Parent A's net expense effect is £6,000: £12,000 software expense less £6,000 recharge income.

Step 3: record the receiving companies’ entries

Subsidiaries B and C: recharged cost
Company and accountDebitCredit
B: software expense£3,600—
B: intercompany payable to A—£3,600
C: software expense£2,400—
C: intercompany payable to A—£2,400

Each receiving company now bears its allocated cost. Both sides of each intercompany relationship have been recorded.

These tables show the accounting effect. An invoice-and-bill workflow may create the same entries through customer and supplier accounts identified by counterparty. Do not post a second manual journal for an entry already created by an invoice or bill.

Step 4: reconcile the intercompany balances

Before settlement, the reciprocal positions should be:

Reciprocal balances before settlement
RelationshipReceivable in APayable in subsidiaryDifference
A and B£3,600£3,600£0
A and C£2,400£2,400£0

Check the underlying transactions as well as the totals. Equal balances can conceal offsetting errors or duplicated entries.

If B has not recorded its bill, A's receivable will exist without the corresponding payable. Investigate and correct the omission in the appropriate period.

See the Intercompany reconciliation guide for the full matching and investigation process.

Step 5: eliminate internal activity on consolidation

Assuming these balances remain outstanding, the group records the following consolidation adjustments:

Group consolidation adjustments
Consolidation adjustmentDebitCredit
Intercompany payables£6,000—
Intercompany receivables—£6,000
Intercompany recharge income£6,000—
Software expense arising from internal recharges—£6,000

Before elimination, the combined company records contain £18,000 of software expense and £6,000 of internal recharge income. After elimination, the group reports the original £12,000 external software expense, with no internal recharge income or reciprocal balance.

These are group-level adjustments. They do not remove the transactions from the individual companies' ledgers.

IFRS 10 paragraph B86(c) requires the elimination of intragroup balances and transactions in consolidated financial statements. Groups should apply their own applicable reporting framework and policies.

Worked example: management services with a mark-up

Worked example

Suppose a service company incurs £20,000 of eligible costs providing management services to a subsidiary. Its established transfer-pricing policy requires a 5% mark-up for this arrangement.

Calculating the intercompany charge
CalculationAmount
Eligible cost base allocated to the subsidiary£20,000
Mark-up at 5%£1,000
Intercompany charge£21,000

The 5% is an illustration, not a recommended or universally acceptable rate. This example again excludes VAT and other taxes and assumes the service is expensed in the period.

Entries in the service company and subsidiary
Company and accountDebitCredit
Service company: intercompany receivable£21,000—
Service company: management service income—£21,000
Subsidiary: management service expense£21,000—
Subsidiary: intercompany payable—£21,000

The original £20,000 of external costs remains in the service company's books. On consolidation, eliminate the £21,000 internal income and corresponding expense, together with any outstanding reciprocal balance. The group retains the original external costs.

Where costs are capitalised into inventory or another asset, additional consolidation adjustments may be needed, including removal of unrealised internal profit. That requires separate analysis.

Should intercompany recharges use invoices, bills or journals?

The appropriate posting method depends on the transaction, documentation requirements, tax treatment, accounting policies and connected systems.

Posting methods for intercompany recharges
MethodPractical consideration
Invoice in the charging company and bill in the receiving companyProvides transaction documents and supports customer/supplier workflows, payment terms and settlement tracking.
Corresponding journalsMay suit certain accounting allocations or adjustments where permitted and appropriate. Journals do not replace required invoices or tax documentation.

Whichever method is used, retain a common reference and ensure both companies record the correct counterparty, amount, currency and period.

Does an intercompany recharge include VAT or a mark-up?

Neither treatment should be assumed solely because the companies belong to the same group.

VAT, GST, sales tax, withholding tax and transfer-pricing requirements depend on the arrangement and relevant jurisdictions. A recharge at cost is not automatically outside the scope of tax. Obtain the appropriate advice and configure the accounting process to implement the resulting policy consistently.

How do intercompany recharges work across currencies?

For a cross-currency recharge, establish the transaction currency and amount, then record each company's functional-currency equivalent under its accounting policies.

Keep the original currency amount, exchange rates, relevant dates and settlement details available for reconciliation.

Two companies may agree on the underlying invoice while reporting different functional-currency amounts. Investigate currency effects separately from missing or incorrect transactions; do not post an unexplained adjustment simply to force balances to match.

What controls should be in place before posting a recharge?

A repeatable recharge process should answer these questions:

  • ?

    Source: Which supplier bills, payroll costs or other transactions are included?

  • ?

    Beneficiary: Which companies received the service or benefit?

  • ?

    Allocation: Is the driver supportable, current and correctly applied?

  • ?

    Pricing: Does the cost base and any mark-up follow the agreed policy?

  • ?

    Period: Are both sides recorded in the appropriate accounting period?

  • ?

    Completeness: Has every receiving company recorded its corresponding entry?

  • ?

    Duplication: Has any source cost or recharge been included twice?

  • ?

    Evidence: Can a reviewer reproduce the calculation and trace it to the postings?

Retain the source documents, allocation calculation, relevant agreement, review evidence and posting references. Automation reduces repetitive work, but the source selection and rules still need review.

Can intercompany recharges be automated in Xero and QuickBooks Online?

Connected intercompany software can automate recurring calculations and corresponding postings across separate Xero organisations or QuickBooks Online companies. The available transaction types and workflow depend on the application and integration.

A controlled workflow connects source transactions, allocation rules, pricing, reciprocal postings and reconciliation. Exceptions should remain visible when a transaction fails to post or a counterparty entry is missing.

As the group grows, this can reduce repeated spreadsheet calculations and separate data entry across individual ledgers.

Frequently asked questions about intercompany recharges

What is the journal entry for an intercompany recharge?

In a simple expense-recharge example, the charging company debits an intercompany receivable and credits recharge income or the appropriate cost-recovery account. The receiving company debits the relevant expense and credits an intercompany payable. The exact treatment depends on the transaction and accounting policy.

What is the difference between an intercompany recharge and transfer pricing?

A recharge is the transaction recorded between group companies. Transfer pricing determines how related-party transactions should be priced. A recharge may implement a transfer-pricing policy through its cost base, allocation and mark-up.

Can one company recharge costs to several subsidiaries?

Yes. Allocate the eligible cost between the benefiting subsidiaries using an appropriate basis, then record the corresponding charge in each counterparty. The calculation should identify the portion retained by the paying company as well as the amounts recharged.

Do intercompany recharges affect group profit?

For services expensed in the period within the consolidated group, internal recharge income and expense are eliminated. The group’s external costs remain. Capitalised costs and unrealised internal profits can require additional adjustments.

Do recharges need to be reconciled if both entries are automated?

Yes. Coordinated posting reduces some sources of mismatch, but reconciliation still checks completeness and identifies subsequent changes, duplicates, failed postings, settlement differences and currency effects.

Does consolidation elimination clear an intercompany debt?

No. Elimination removes internal balances from consolidated reporting. Payment or another valid settlement mechanism clears the debt in the companies’ own records.

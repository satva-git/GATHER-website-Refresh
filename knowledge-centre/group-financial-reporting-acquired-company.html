How to consolidate an acquired company

A practical guide to consolidating an acquired company, covering acquisition dates, goodwill, opening balances and post-acquisition group reporting.

On this page

Consolidating an acquired company starts with the date control is obtained, not simply the date its ledger is connected.

The finance team needs a supported acquisition balance sheet, the correct post-acquisition results and a clear record of the adjustments that turn the acquired company’s accounts into group figures. This guide explains the practical work for acquisitive groups using Xero and QuickBooks Online.

Establish what was acquired and when

Confirm the legal transaction, the accounting acquirer and whether the acquired activities constitute a business under the reporting framework. A business combination, an asset purchase and a transaction under common control can require different accounting. Do not apply a standard acquisition template before resolving that distinction.

For an IFRS business combination within IFRS 3, the acquisition date is the date control is obtained. Signing, completion and payment dates may differ. Retain the evidence supporting the date used and assess substantive rights as well as the shareholding.

This article uses a straightforward acquisition of a business as its example. Local GAAP can differ, including the subsequent treatment of goodwill.

Prepare the acquisition information pack

Collect the share purchase agreement, consideration schedule, ownership details and acquisition-date financial information. Reconcile the opening balances to the acquired company’s records and investigate material gaps before building the consolidation.

Identify the acquired assets and liabilities and determine the recognition and measurement adjustments required. The entity’s book values are a starting point; they are not automatically the amounts used for acquisition accounting.

  • ✓

    Separate cash paid, deferred amounts and contingent consideration for assessment.

  • ✓

    Document fair-value adjustments and supporting valuations.

  • ✓

    Consider identifiable intangible assets and associated deferred tax.

  • ✓

    Record any non-controlling interests and the measurement basis selected where a choice is permitted.

A simple goodwill calculation

Worked example

Assume the parent acquires 100% of a business for £1,200,000 cash. The acquisition-date book net assets are £700,000. A supported property fair-value uplift is £100,000, with a related £25,000 deferred tax liability. Assume these are the only acquisition adjustments and all amounts qualify for recognition.

Acquisition calculation
Acquisition calculationAmount
Book net assets£700,000
Property fair-value uplift£100,000
Related deferred tax liability(£25,000)
Identifiable net assets recognised£775,000
Consideration transferred£1,200,000
Goodwill£425,000

The £425,000 difference is goodwill in this simplified full-acquisition example. The working papers should preserve the inputs and calculation. A balancing number without that support does not explain what was acquired.

The consolidation offsets the investment recorded by the parent against the relevant acquired equity and recognises the acquisition adjustments. It does not retain both the investment and the subsidiary’s net assets as separate group assets.

Separate pre-acquisition and post-acquisition performance

Example

Assume control is obtained on 1 July and the group reports to 31 December. The consolidated income statement includes the subsidiary’s results from 1 July, adjusted to the group accounting basis. It does not include the subsidiary’s January to June trading as current-year group performance.

Obtain an actual acquisition-date cut-off wherever practical. A simple time apportionment may misstate the result if trading is seasonal, a significant contract completed or a material one-off item occurred.

The closing balance sheet still includes the controlled subsidiary’s relevant assets and liabilities at the reporting date. The treatment of pre-acquisition equity and post-acquisition results must therefore be connected.

Bring the acquired ledger into the group structure

Map local accounts to the group reporting template without losing the detail needed to explain opening balances and subsequent movements. Check new account types, accounting policies, currency and reporting dates.

Keep acquisition entries identifiable. A fair-value uplift may create future depreciation or amortisation adjustments; recording only the opening uplift can overstate later profit.

If an existing group company starts trading with the acquired business, identify those transactions from the date they become intragroup. Avoid eliminating genuine external transactions from before acquisition merely because the counterparty is now a subsidiary.

Build the recurring consolidation process

Create a roll-forward for goodwill, fair-value adjustments, deferred tax and post-acquisition reserves. Record which calculations change each month and which inputs remain fixed unless new information requires reassessment.

Under IFRS, goodwill is subject to impairment requirements rather than routine amortisation. Other frameworks may require amortisation. The system configuration must follow the framework selected by the group.

For a partial acquisition, add NCI calculations. For a staged acquisition or later ownership change, reassess the accounting rather than reusing the original full-acquisition example.

Review the first consolidated close

Check that the ownership schedule, acquisition date and consideration agree to supporting documents. Reconcile acquisition-date net assets and the opening consolidation entries before reviewing the current period.

Confirm that the consolidated result includes the correct trading period, the investment elimination is complete and the closing balance sheet reflects the acquisition adjustments. Explain material movements separately from underlying trading.

The best handover from transaction accounting to monthly reporting leaves a documented opening position and a repeatable process for every subsequent period.

Frequently asked questions

Do we consolidate from the date we pay for the shares?

Not necessarily. For an IFRS business combination, use the date control is obtained. Payment and legal milestones need to be assessed against the facts.

Can we use the acquired company’s year-to-date profit?

Only the appropriate post-acquisition results belong in group performance. Obtain a reliable split at the acquisition date.

Is goodwill always purchase price less book equity?

No. Identifiable assets and liabilities can need recognition and measurement adjustments, and NCI or previously held interests can affect the calculation.

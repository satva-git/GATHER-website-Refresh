How to record and reconcile intercompany loans in Xero and QuickBooks Online

An intercompany loan creates a receivable in the lender’s books and a payable in the borrower’s books. Both entities should agree on the outstanding amount in the loan currency. When they use different functional currencies, the finance team also needs to check that each side has been valued correctly at month end.

GATHER.nexus tracks intercompany loan balances throughout the month, automatically matches corresponding transactions and keeps unresolved differences visible. When an FX mismatch needs a correcting journal, the finance team can post it centrally from GATHER.nexus and sync it to the relevant Xero or QuickBooks Online ledger. That lets the team fix the underlying balance before it reaches the consolidation working papers. See how central correction works in GATHER.nexus.

How do you record an intercompany loan?

Set up a loan receivable account for the lender and a corresponding loan payable account for the borrower.

Use consistent counterparty names and loan references across both ledgers, especially where the same entities have multiple loans or trade balances.

EventLenderBorrower
£100,000 drawdownDebit intercompany loan receivable £100,000. Credit bank £100,000.Debit bank £100,000. Credit intercompany loan payable £100,000.
£500 interest accruedDebit intercompany interest receivable £500. Credit interest income £500.Debit interest expense £500. Credit intercompany interest payable £500.
£20,000 principal repaymentDebit bank £20,000. Credit intercompany loan receivable £20,000.Debit intercompany loan payable £20,000. Credit bank £20,000.

In QuickBooks Online, intercompany balances are sometimes labelled Due from and Due to.

The table shows the entries for a £100,000 loan between two GBP entities.

After these entries, both ledgers should show £80,000 of outstanding principal and £500 of accrued interest.

Tracking those amounts separately makes it easier to identify whether a difference comes from funding, a repayment or an interest accrual.

Worked example of an FX mismatch on an intercompany loan

A UK parent lends £100,000 to a US subsidiary. The loan is denominated in GBP, the parent’s functional currency is GBP and the subsidiary’s functional currency is USD.

Reconciliation checkUK parentUS subsidiary
Outstanding principal in the loan currency£100,000 receivable£100,000 payable
Functional-currency balance at drawdown, at $1.25 per £1£100,000$125,000
Expected balance at month end, at $1.28 per £1£100,000$128,000
Balance recorded before correction£100,000$125,000

Assume there are no repayments or interest in this example.

Both companies agree that £100,000 is outstanding. The reconciliation difference comes from the US subsidiary still carrying the liability at the drawdown rate.

US subsidiary journalDebitCredit
FX loss$3,000
Intercompany loan payable$3,000

At the closing rate, its $125,000 balance translates to approximately £97,656, leaving an apparent mismatch of £2,344 against the parent’s £100,000 receivable.

Once the team confirms the subsidiary hasn’t already recorded the revaluation, it can post the $3,000 correcting journal centrally from GATHER.nexus to the subsidiary’s connected ledger.

The subsidiary’s payable is now $128,000, which translates to £100,000 at the closing rate. The two principal balances agree and can be eliminated on consolidation. Under IAS 21, the genuine exchange difference generally remains in consolidated profit or loss.

A qualifying loan that forms part of the net investment in a foreign operation can have a different treatment. IAS 21, paragraphs 32–33 and 45.

How GATHER.nexus tracks and resolves intercompany loan differences

A repayment booked on one side only, a late interest accrual and a missed FX revaluation can all show up as a difference between the same two companies. GATHER.nexus gives the group finance team one place to follow the balance and investigate the transactions behind it.

  1. Spot the difference during the month. The counterparty matrix shows which entity pairs have outstanding differences. The timeline shows when a particular pair moved out of balance, narrowing the search to the relevant period.
  2. Review the unmatched transactions. GATHER.nexus continuously matches corresponding entries and keeps unmatched items visible. The team can check whether the difference is due to a missing entry, timing, an incorrect amount or FX.
  3. Post the FX correction centrally. Once the team confirms the cause and amount, it can post the correcting journal from GATHER.nexus and sync it to the relevant Xero or QuickBooks Online ledger. In the example above, the correction goes to the US subsidiary, where the loan payable needs to be revalued.
  4. Validate the corrected position against the consolidation working papers. Check that the reconciled principal and any accrued interest agree with the amounts being eliminated before signing off group reporting.

Frequently asked questions

Why don’t intercompany loan balances agree?

Common causes include a drawdown or repayment recorded by only one entity, different posting dates, an interest accrual missing from one side, or a missed FX revaluation. For a foreign-currency loan, compare the outstanding principal in the loan currency first. Then check the functional-currency carrying amounts and exchange rates.

Can I post an intercompany FX correction without opening each Xero or QuickBooks Online company?

Yes. GATHER.nexus lets the group finance team post a correcting journal centrally and sync it to the relevant connected ledger. The team can then review the updated counterparty position in the same reconciliation workflow.

What gets eliminated on consolidation?

Eliminate the reciprocal loan receivable and payable, along with any corresponding accrued interest balances and internal interest income and expense. Under IAS 21, account separately for genuine exchange differences arising from the loan.

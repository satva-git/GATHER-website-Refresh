How does manual intercompany accounting compare to the intercompany process with GATHER.nexus?

The challenge with intercompany accounting is the number of separate steps required to complete the process. Use the contents to jump to recharges, reconciliation, multi-entry posting, or the Group workflow.

Overview

A cost may need to be identified, allocated between entities, adjusted for transfer pricing, converted into another currency, posted into two accounting systems, reconciled at month end and ultimately eliminated on consolidation.

When these processes are managed manually, action and documentation is often spread across Xero or QuickBooks Online, spreadsheets, journals, emails and consolidation working papers.

GATHER.nexus takes a different approach. Rather than replacing Xero or QuickBooks Online as the underlying accounting systems, GATHER provides a multi-entity finance layer above them, bringing intercompany recharges, reconciliations and Group reporting into a connected workflow.

Manual vs automated intercompany recharges

Intercompany recharges can become surprisingly labour-intensive.

Consider a Group where the parent company pays for software, insurance, marketing and central employees on behalf of its subsidiaries. 

The finance team may need to identify the relevant costs, determine which companies should bear them, calculate allocations, apply transfer-pricing mark-ups and then create the corresponding accounting entries across multiple entities.

This becomes increasingly valuable as the number of entities, currencies, allocation rules and intercompany relationships grows.

Manual vs automated intercompany reconciliation

Recharges are only one side of the problem. At some point, the finance team also needs to demonstrate that amounts recorded between Group companies actually agree.

In a traditional month-end process, this often means exporting balances from individual accounting systems, combining them in Excel and investigating differences one by one.

A fundamental difference is when problems are identified. A spreadsheet-based reconciliation process often discovers intercompany differences during the month-end close.

A more continuous intercompany control process allows differences to be identified and investigated during the accounting period, before they become a consolidation problem.

Why intercompany accounting creates a multi-entry problem

One reason intercompany accounting creates so much manual work is that a single commercial event can require accounting entries across more than one legal entity.

Worked example

Imagine UK Parent incurs £10,000 of software costs and determines that £4,000 should be recharged to US Subsidiary. Economically, this is one recharge. From an accounting perspective, however, the Group needs corresponding entries on both sides of the transaction.

Traditional processes often treat the two companies as separate accounting exercises and subsequently try to reconcile them.

GATHER can instead treat the recharge as one economic event that needs to be reflected correctly across both entities. This is the principle behind GATHER’s quadruple-entry approach to intercompany recharges.

GATHER doesn’t replace Xero or QuickBooks Online. They remain the underlying accounting ledgers for the individual companies.

Instead, GATHER provides the multi-entity finance layer above them. That allows the Group finance team to manage processes that inherently cross legal-entity boundaries without repeatedly moving data between individual accounting systems and spreadsheets.

Why this matters as a Group grows

For a Group with two entities and relatively few intercompany transactions, a spreadsheet may be perfectly adequate.

The problem is that intercompany complexity doesn’t necessarily increase in a straight line. Adding more entities can create more potential counterparty relationships. 

Adding currencies introduces FX differences. Shared services create allocation requirements. International expansion can introduce transfer-pricing considerations. Acquisitions can add different charts of accounts and accounting practices.

The finance team can therefore find itself maintaining an increasingly sophisticated intercompany accounting system except that the system is actually a collection of spreadsheets, manual journals and month-end procedures.

For multi-entity Groups using Xero and QuickBooks Online, this operational and reporting complexity is the problem GATHER.nexus is designed to solve.

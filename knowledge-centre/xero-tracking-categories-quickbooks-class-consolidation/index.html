How to Consolidate Xero Tracking Categories and QuickBooks Classes Across Multiple Entities

Consolidate Xero tracking categories and QuickBooks classes with GATHER.nexus group class roll-ups and source-level intercompany eliminations.

What is tracking category and class consolidation?

Tracking category and class consolidation combines financial information from multiple accounting entities into consistent group-level reporting dimensions. Xero uses tracking categories and tracking options; QuickBooks Online uses classes. When subsidiaries have different names and structures, finance teams need a common way to report across departments, divisions, business units and regions.

GATHER.nexus maps entity-level Xero tracking categories and QuickBooks Online classes into consistent group classes. Intercompany eliminations use the underlying Xero tracking categories and QuickBooks classes - not the GATHER group class roll-up. The effects of those adjustments then flow through the mapping into consolidated group class reports.

The CFO may want to see Commercial, Operations and Corporate across the group, even though subsidiaries use different labels. Without mapping, teams often export separate reports and manually reclassify transactions. This creates inconsistent definitions, spreadsheet maintenance, errors and difficulty comparing divisions - especially after acquisitions.

2. What is a GATHER.nexus Group Class?

A Group Class is a common reporting classification used across legal entities. It lets subsidiaries retain their local accounting classifications while their financial results are presented using a standardised group structure.

3. How GATHER.nexus handles Group Class mapping and roll-ups

Map local classifications

Xero tracking options and QuickBooks Online classes are mapped to common GATHER Group Classes. For example, Xero Sales and QuickBooks Commercial can both contribute to group Commercial.

Roll up across entities

Mapped financial results can be aggregated across multiple entities. A Group Class can therefore show combined performance without manual assembly of separate ledger reports.

Report through management dimensions

The resulting consolidated reports can answer questions such as: “How profitable is our Commercial division across the group?” This complements, rather than replaces, reporting by legal entity.

4. Why intercompany eliminations matter in divisional reporting

Internal recharges create revenue in one group company and costs in another. Both may be valid in local accounts, but consolidated reporting generally eliminates internal transactions. Without elimination attribution to the relevant reporting classifications, a divisional P&L can overstate one division’s revenue and another’s costs even when total group profit is unchanged.

5. How eliminations and group class reporting work together

  1. Source tracking category/class
  2. Source-classification elimination
  3. Group class mapping
  4. Consolidated report

Step 1: Retain source classifications

The relevant Xero tracking categories/options and QuickBooks Online classes remain the source classifications for accounting and reporting analysis.

Step 2: Attribute eliminations using Xero/QBO concepts

Intercompany elimination adjustments are handled using those underlying source classifications. They are not calculated by first rolling transactions up into GATHER Group Classes.

Step 3: Map to GATHER Group Classes

Local classifications, including the classifications relevant to elimination adjustments, map into the group reporting structure.

Step 4: Roll up adjusted results

Consolidated Group Class reports reflect the relevant elimination effects. Source classification determines elimination attribution; GATHER Group Class determines reporting aggregation and presentation.

7. Why source-level eliminations are different from roll-ups

Simply combining local classifications is a reporting operation. Eliminating intercompany activity is a consolidation accounting operation. A tool that aggregates classes does not necessarily attribute elimination adjustments to those classes. GATHER.nexus connects these two processes while keeping their functions distinct: eliminations use source classifications; Group Classes provide a consistent presentation layer.

9. From legal-entity reporting to management reporting

Group reporting often needs to show profitability by function, business unit, geography or department, rather than solely by legal entity. Group Class mapping allows these views to cross subsidiary boundaries. Reflecting source-classification eliminations in those roll-ups makes the results more useful for management decisions.

Frequently asked questions

Can Xero tracking categories be consolidated across companies?

Yes. GATHER.nexus can map Xero tracking options from different organisations into common Group Classes for consolidated reporting.

Can QuickBooks Online classes be consolidated across companies?

Yes. QuickBooks Online classes can be mapped into GATHER Group Classes across multiple entities.

Can Xero tracking categories and QuickBooks classes appear in the same consolidated report?

Yes. GATHER.nexus supports mixed Xero and QuickBooks Online groups and a common Group Class reporting structure.

What is the difference between a Xero tracking category and a GATHER Group Class?

A Xero tracking category is a dimension in an individual Xero organisation; a GATHER Group Class is a group-wide reporting classification into which local tracking options or classes can map.

How does GATHER.nexus handle intercompany eliminations when reporting by group class?

Eliminations use underlying Xero tracking categories and QuickBooks Online classes. Their effects flow into reports through the separate mapping of those classifications to GATHER Group Classes; the eliminations are not calculated at the Group Class roll-up level.

Why might consolidated divisional results differ from the sum of company class reports?

They can reflect classification mapping, intercompany eliminations, currency translation and other consolidation adjustments.

Do subsidiaries need identical tracking categories or classes?

No. Local classifications can differ and still map into a consistent Group Class structure.

Can eliminations affect different Group Classes?

Yes. The two sides of an intercompany transaction can have different source classifications, which may map to different Group Classes. The corresponding elimination effects can then appear in those Group Class results.

Conclusion: Consolidated reporting needs to go beyond legal entities

Reliable multi-entity management reporting needs both a common reporting structure and correctly attributed consolidation adjustments. GATHER.nexus maps Xero tracking categories and QuickBooks Online classes into Group Classes, while handling intercompany eliminations using the underlying source classifications. The resulting reports combine consistent group-wide presentation with the effects of source-level elimination accounting.

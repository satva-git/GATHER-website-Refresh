Intercompany Reconciliation

7 questions

  • An intercompany reconciliation compares transactions and balances recorded between companies within the same Group. The objective is to ensure that amounts recorded by one entity agree with the corresponding amounts recorded by the counterparty before the Group accounts are consolidated.

  • Intercompany reconciliations identify missing transactions, timing differences, foreign exchange differences and posting errors before consolidation. Resolving these differences improves the accuracy of Group reporting and makes intercompany eliminations more reliable.

  • Start by identifying reciprocal balances between Group companies, compare the underlying transactions, match corresponding entries and investigate any differences. Missing or incorrect entries are then corrected before the balances are approved for consolidation.

  • For active multi-entity Groups, intercompany accounts should ideally be monitored throughout the month rather than reconciled only at month end. Weekly or continuous reconciliation allows differences to be identified earlier and can significantly reduce pressure during the close.

  • Common causes include missing transactions, different posting dates, incorrect amounts, transactions recorded in different periods, duplicate entries, inconsistent account coding and foreign exchange differences.

  • Reconciliation ensures that transactions and balances recorded by two Group companies agree. Elimination removes the corresponding intercompany income, expenses, assets and liabilities when the consolidated financial statements are prepared. Reconcile first; eliminate second.

  • The difference should be investigated before consolidation. Finance teams typically drill into the underlying transactions to identify missing entries, timing differences, FX differences or posting errors and then make the necessary corrections.

Intercompany Recharges

7 questions

  • An intercompany recharge occurs when one Group company allocates a cost, service or expense to another Group company. Examples include management costs, payroll, software licences, rent, insurance, IT services and marketing expenditure.

  • The company initially incurring the cost determines how much should be allocated to other Group entities. The recharge is then recorded in both the charging entity and receiving entity, typically through journals or intercompany invoices and bills.

  • Common examples include central management costs, finance and HR functions, technology costs, software subscriptions, property costs, insurance, marketing, professional fees and shared employees. The appropriate treatment depends on the nature of the cost and the Group’s accounting and transfer-pricing policies.

  • An intercompany allocation distributes a cost across multiple Group companies using a defined basis. For example, a £100,000 central software licence might be allocated between subsidiaries according to employee numbers, usage or another appropriate allocation driver.

  • Yes. Intercompany software can automate recurring recharge calculations, allocation rules, mark-ups and the corresponding entries in each company’s accounting system. This removes much of the manual journal preparation traditionally performed in spreadsheets.

  • Sometimes. Certain intercompany services may require an arm’s-length mark-up under the Group’s transfer-pricing policy and applicable tax rules. The appropriate treatment depends on the transaction, jurisdictions involved and relevant tax requirements.

  • A recharge generally reallocates specific costs incurred on behalf of another entity, whereas a management fee typically charges for management or central services provided to another Group company. In practice, the terminology and accounting treatment can vary between Groups.

Xero

4 questions

  • Each legal entity normally has its own Xero organisation, so reciprocal transactions must ultimately be reflected in both sets of books. Groups can manage this manually or use specialist multi-entity software, such as GATHER.nexus, to coordinate recharges, reconciliation and Group reporting across their Xero organisations.

  • Finance teams operating separate Xero organisations may use specialist intercompany software, such as GATHER.nexus to bring the relevant information together, match transactions and highlight differences across entities rather than relying on spreadsheets to perform the reconciliation.

  • Third-party intercompany platforms can automate the calculation and posting of corresponding transactions across connected Xero organisations, subject to the workflow and posting methods supported by the software.

  • As the number of entities grows, it becomes increasingly useful to have a multi-entity layer above the individual Xero organisations. This can provide centralised intercompany control, reconciliation, Group reporting, consolidation and planning without replacing Xero as the underlying accounting ledger.

QuickBooks Online

4 questions

  • Groups using separate QuickBooks Online companies need a process for ensuring that transactions between entities are recorded consistently on both sides. Specialist multi-entity software can centralise the workflow and reduce reliance on manual journals and spreadsheets.

  • Specialist multi-entity applications can connect to QuickBooks Online companies and provide additional intercompany reconciliation workflows, including matching balances, identifying differences and managing corrections.

  • Yes. A connected intercompany platform can calculate allocations and create the corresponding accounting entries across the relevant QuickBooks Online companies, depending on the integration and transaction types supported.

  • For larger Groups, a central multi-entity platform can provide visibility across individual QuickBooks companies while adding Group-level processes such as intercompany control, consolidation, reporting and planning.

Multi-Currency and Consolidation

4 questions

  • Multi-currency reconciliation requires the underlying transactions to be matched while separately identifying differences caused by exchange rates. Finance teams then determine whether differences represent genuine accounting errors, timing differences or FX movements requiring appropriate accounting treatment.

  • The two entities may use different exchange rates or record transactions and settlements on different dates. This can create differences even when both companies have correctly recorded the underlying transaction in their respective functional currencies.

  • They should. If reciprocal balances do not agree, the corresponding elimination may leave unexplained residual amounts in the consolidated accounts. A controlled close process therefore normally reconciles intercompany positions before completing eliminations.

  • Yes. Integrating the two processes allows validated intercompany balances to flow into the consolidation process, where the corresponding balances and transactions can then be eliminated. This creates a more controlled workflow than treating reconciliation and consolidation as separate spreadsheet exercises.

4 questions

Depending on the platform, functionality can include automated recharges, reciprocal postings, transaction matching, reconciliation, FX management, approvals, audit trails and integration with consolidation.

  • Look for multi-entity and multi-currency support, automated matching, transaction-level drill-down, exception management, correction workflows, audit trails and integration with your accounting and consolidation systems. For Xero and QuickBooks Groups, the quality of the underlying integrations is particularly important.

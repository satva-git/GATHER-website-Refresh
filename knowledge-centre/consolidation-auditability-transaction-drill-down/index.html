Why auditability and transaction-level drill-down are essential for Xero and QuickBooks consolidation

End-to-end auditability and transaction-level drill-down help finance teams explain how consolidated figures were produced, verify the underlying transactions and substantiate group accounting adjustments.

End-to-end auditability and transaction-level drill-down help finance teams explain how consolidated figures were produced, verify the underlying transactions and substantiate group accounting adjustments. For multi-entity groups using Xero or QuickBooks Online, these capabilities are essential for an efficient close, effective financial review and dependable group reporting.

Consolidation brings together more than the balances of individual companies. It introduces account mappings, currency translation, intercompany eliminations and group-level adjustments.

Each step can affect the reported result. Finance teams therefore need to see both the underlying entity data and the consolidation accounting applied to it.

A dependable consolidation platform should make it straightforward to answer: where did this number come from, how was it calculated, and what evidence supports it?

What is end-to-end auditability in multi-entity consolidation?

End-to-end auditability is the ability to trace a consolidated figure through the data, calculations and accounting treatments that produced it.

For a Xero or QuickBooks Online group, this means understanding:

  • Which entities and reporting periods contribute to the figure.
  • How entity ledger accounts map into group reporting categories.
  • How foreign-currency balances have been translated.
  • Which intercompany transactions and balances have been eliminated.
  • Which consolidation journals and topside adjustments have been applied.
  • What calculations and explanations support those adjustments.

The objective is to give a reviewer enough evidence to understand the reported figure and assess whether its accounting treatment is appropriate.

What is transaction-level drill-down?

Transaction-level drill-down is the ability to move from a summary financial figure to the individual accounting entries that contribute to it.

In multi-entity consolidation, a typical investigation follows this path:

Consolidated reporting line → entity contribution → general ledger account → individual transaction.

For example, a controller investigating group professional fees should be able to identify the relevant subsidiary, inspect its legal expenses account and review the transactions driving the movement.

Where supported, click-through to the original Xero or QuickBooks Online record allows further investigation within the source accounting system.

How does drill-down differ from an audit trail?

Drill-down and audit trails support different parts of financial review.

CapabilityQuestion it helps answer
Transaction-level drill-downWhich transactions make up this balance?
Consolidation calculation trailHow was this group adjustment calculated?
Journal historyWhich accounting entries affected the reported figure?
Administrative activity logWho changed a setting or record, and when?
Period review evidenceWhat was reviewed and completed before reporting?

A platform may provide some of these capabilities without providing all of them. Finance teams should assess each explicitly.

Seeing the transactions behind an entity balance does not, by itself, explain a goodwill calculation or an intercompany elimination. Likewise, an activity log does not replace supporting accounting calculations.

Why are these capabilities crucial for Xero and QuickBooks groups?

Multi-entity reporting requires finance teams to validate two connected layers: the underlying entity accounting and the consolidation accounting applied above it.

A source transaction can be correct but mapped to the wrong group reporting category. An entity balance can be accurate but translated using an inappropriate rate. An elimination can be posted without resolving an underlying counterparty difference.

End-to-end auditability helps reviewers identify where an issue arose. Transaction-level drill-down helps them investigate the detail needed to resolve it.

These capabilities become increasingly valuable as groups add entities, currencies, acquisitions and reporting requirements.

1. Explain financial movements faster

A consolidated reporting pack often prompts questions such as:

  • Why has gross margin fallen?
  • Which subsidiary caused the increase in overheads?
  • What sits behind the movement in other receivables?
  • Does this month’s result include an unusual transaction?

Transaction-level drill-down gives finance teams a direct route to the evidence.

Suppose consolidated professional fees increase by £45,000. Entity-level detail identifies the subsidiary responsible. Ledger account detail narrows the movement to legal costs. Transaction-level detail reveals an acquisition-related invoice.

The finance team can then assess the accounting treatment and prepare an informed explanation.

Without that connected route, the same investigation may require exports from several ledgers, spreadsheet analysis and repeated reconciliations back to the group report.

2. Make group adjustments transparent

Some consolidated figures originate in the group accounting process rather than in an individual entity’s ledger.

Examples include:

  • Intercompany eliminations.
  • Foreign exchange differences in reserves.
  • Goodwill and fair value adjustments.
  • Non-controlling interests.
  • Group accounting policy adjustments.
  • Elimination of unrealised intercompany profit.

These adjustments need supporting journals, calculations and explanations.

A foreign exchange differences in reserves entry should have a calculation that a reviewer can inspect. An acquisition adjustment should connect to the relevant ownership assumptions and acquisition accounting inputs.

The test is whether another qualified finance professional can follow the evidence and understand how the adjustment was produced.

3. Connect intercompany differences with their consolidation treatment

Intercompany reconciliation and intercompany elimination address related requirements.

Reconciliation establishes whether counterparties agree and explains differences. Elimination removes the relevant intragroup activity and balances from consolidated reporting.

Suppose one subsidiary records a £100,000 intercompany receivable while its counterparty records a £96,000 payable, expressed in a common currency for comparison.

The £4,000 difference may arise from timing, a missing posting, currency treatment or an accounting error.

Transaction-level detail helps identify the cause. An auditable consolidation process shows how the difference was corrected or treated at group level.

Connecting reconciliation evidence with consolidation working papers helps reviewers assess whether the elimination is supported by the underlying counterparty position.

4. Preserve knowledge across the finance team

Consolidation processes often depend on the person who knows why a mapping was created, how an adjustment works or which spreadsheet contains the supporting calculation.

That dependence makes handovers harder and can delay reporting when someone is unavailable.

Maintaining calculations, journals and explanations within a structured workflow makes the consolidation easier to review and repeat. New team members can understand prior treatments, and reviewers can revisit earlier periods with less reliance on memory.

For outsourced finance teams, the same principle supports consistency across multiple client groups.

5. Support audit and due diligence

External auditors, lenders and prospective investors may ask how a reported figure was derived and what supports the accounting treatment.

An accessible evidence trail helps finance teams respond with entity contributions, source transactions, consolidation journals and backing calculations.

Historical reporting also deserves attention. If an underlying transaction, mapping or assumption changes after a report has been issued, the finance team needs to explain the difference between the issued figures and the current position.

When evaluating software, ask how it handles data refreshes, changes and completed reporting periods.

Auditability helps organise and substantiate financial information. Professional judgement remains necessary to assess the quality of the records and the appropriateness of the accounting.

What should you look for in a consolidation platform?

Ask the provider to demonstrate a realistic investigation from a consolidated report through to its supporting evidence.

RequirementWhat to check
Entity visibilityCan you identify each entity’s contribution to a group reporting line?
Account detailCan you inspect the underlying general ledger accounts?
Transaction detailCan you review the individual transactions supporting a balance?
Source record accessCan you navigate to the relevant Xero or QuickBooks Online record?
Adjustment evidenceCan you inspect group journals and their backing calculations?
Intercompany alignmentCan you connect reconciliation findings with consolidation treatment?
Historical integrityCan you explain changes affecting previously issued figures?
Review workflowCan reviewers establish what has been completed before reporting?

For groups using both Xero and QuickBooks Online, ask the provider to demonstrate these capabilities across both accounting systems.

Frequently asked questions

Why is transaction-level drill-down important in consolidation software?

Transaction-level drill-down helps finance teams identify the accounting entries behind consolidated balances. It supports variance analysis, error investigation and financial review by connecting group reporting with underlying entity transactions.

Is entity-level drill-down enough?

Entity-level drill-down shows which company contributes to a figure. Finance teams often also need ledger account and transaction detail to explain the movement or investigate its accounting treatment.

Does an audit trail guarantee that consolidated accounts are correct?

An audit trail provides evidence of how figures were produced. Finance teams must still assess data completeness, mappings, accounting policies, calculations and judgements.

Why do consolidation journals need backing calculations?

Consolidation journals can materially affect reported results without appearing in the underlying entity ledgers. Supporting calculations allow reviewers to understand the inputs, reproduce the treatment and assess the resulting entry.

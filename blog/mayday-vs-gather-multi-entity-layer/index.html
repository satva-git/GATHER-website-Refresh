Mayday vs GATHER.nexus

Month-end close and the multi-entity finance layer

Howard Thompson, FCA, founder of GATHER.nexus · 24 November 2026

On this page

Mayday and close coordination How the strategies compare Organising around the group Where each creates value FAQs

Mayday is a month-end close platform that combines accounting automation with close coordination. GATHER.nexus is a multi-entity finance platform that connects intercompany control, consolidation, group reporting and planning for finance teams using Xero and QuickBooks Online. Both address intercompany work. Their product strategies differ in how far they carry that work and what they organise around.

When a business adds a second legal entity, neither ledger can describe the group on its own. One company’s receivable is another’s payable. The entities may use different charts of accounts or currencies. Each can close its books successfully while group finance still has to build a consolidated view. Where software takes responsibility for that work is a strategic design choice.

Mayday has expanded from task automation to close coordination

Mayday’s early launch highlighted intercompany recharges as a way to make month end less labour-intensive. Its February 2026 acquisition of Easy Month End extended that strategy into close management. Mayday now combines task management and progress tracking with balance sheet reconciliations and automation for intercompany, prepayments, deferred revenue and adjusting journals.

A controller managing several entity closes needs to know who owns each task, which balances have been reconciled and which exceptions could delay sign-off. Mayday’s scope follows that responsibility. It can serve a group controller and also address recurring close tasks that arise in a single-entity business.

GATHER.nexus organises the work around the group

At GATHER.nexus, we use “multi-entity layer” to describe the software that sits above the entity ledgers and manages the accounting and reporting work that arises because a business operates as a group. Xero and QuickBooks Online retain the entity accounts. GATHER.nexus holds the group reporting structure, consolidation journals and working papers that finance uses to produce and review a consolidated result.

The workflow starts before consolidation. Take a UK parent that recharges shared software costs to a US subsidiary. Finance records the recharge on both sides and checks whether the counterparty balances agree. Timing or currency differences may need investigation – this work is undertaken as part of an intercompany reconciliation exercise between the counterparties. During consolidation, finance eliminates the internal income and expense and retains the external software cost. In group planning, it can then compare that cost with budget.

Each stage uses the same underlying event to answer a different accounting question. When spending looks unusual in the group report, finance can drill into working papers, inspect the consolidation adjustment and trace the entity figures down to individual transactions. That continuity reduces the work involved in reconstructing how a number was produced.

Where the two approaches create value

The distinction becomes practical when finance identifies where it loses time. A team chasing reconciliations, recurring journals and entity sign-offs has a close coordination problem. A team spending days after sign-off remapping accounts, applying FX treatments, posting eliminations, ensuring that intercompany counterparties are in balance and rebuilding the budget comparison has a group finance problem. A growing business can face both at once.

The product boundary determines which handoffs finance still has to manage. Mayday’s development shows the value of managing accounting tasks as part of a coordinated close. GATHER.nexus extends the group workflow from the original intercompany event through to the reported result and the decisions that follow. For group finance, that continuity makes the consolidated figures easier to explain and use.

Frequently asked questions

What is the difference between Mayday and GATHER.nexus?

Mayday focuses on automating and coordinating month end. GATHER.nexus focuses on connecting intercompany control (automating intercompany recharges and reconciliations) with consolidation, group reporting and planning across entities.

How does intercompany reconciliation differ from elimination?

Reconciliation checks whether the balances recorded by counterparties agree. Elimination removes internal balances or transactions from the consolidated accounts so the group is presented as a single economic entity.
